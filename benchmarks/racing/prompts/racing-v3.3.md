## 09. Mode: Race vs AI — Riviera Grand Prix

Conduct **three-lap races against five concurrent AI-controlled cars**, six cars on the course including the player. All five opponents must be present, visible in appropriate circumstances, physically and competitively participating, and represented accurately in live classification and the recorded replay.

The intended normal-player race duration is **approximately 2–4 minutes**. The duration is a pacing goal for this mode, not a universal timer for Time Trial or Checkpoint Run.

Offer **three fixed difficulty settings: Easy, Normal, Hard**. Avoid differences that consist exclusively of changing top speed or teleporting rivals to change results. Opponents should have plausible opportunities to win, be overtaken, and make mistakes.

Positions must be derived from valid race progress along the course and completed laps. The player's apparent camera position, distance to finish in a straight line, or a scripted placement list is not adequate. Design a **substantial, complete checkpoint sequence**, not a trivial one-gate or brief single-loop demonstration. It may be an extended point-to-point route or a multi-lap ordered-gate sequence on the dedicated course; make progress toward the final gate easy to understand (for example, `Checkpoint X / N`). Completing the full sequence before time expires succeeds; running out of time fails, with coherent results and restart options. Exact course length, checkpoint count, and reward cadence remain creative decisions.

Combos and scoring should reward meaningful driving actions, potentially including drift angle/duration, high-speed clean sectors, near-misses, apex precision, or nitro control. **You decide the exact bonus economy and interactions.** Do not treat an illustrative combo example as a binding formula, and do not impose an arbitrary fixed number of seconds, points, or multipliers solely to satisfy the prompt. The game must nevertheless avoid trivial infinite-time, infinitely farmable points, or checkpoint-trigger exploits that make progression meaningless.

Checkpoint guidance must be legible while moving. Repeatedly crossing one trigger, skipping a mandatory section, respawning near a future marker, or reversing through checkpoints must not generate unlimited rewards. When a combo is lost or a run ends, provide comprehensible feedback rather than silently changing the score or timer.

## 11. Authoritative simulation state and timing

Use **one coherent source of truth for active-race simulation time and race progression**, with deliberate ownership of player inputs, cars, checkpoints, AI, coupled weather/daylight, damage, nitro, Signature Feature state, and session status. Rendering cadence and UI update frequency must not independently control physical distance, lap time, or AI advancement. A fixed-step simulation with interpolated rendering is one valid method; other stable, frame-rate-independent approaches are allowed.

Driving at different rendering frame rates must not fundamentally change handling, timer speed, checkpoints, or recorded outcome. Constrain pathological frame deltas (including the first frame after asset loads, shader compilation, dynamic IBL cubemap updates, graphics preset switches, window resize, or tab resume). Make start, pause, Photo Mode, resume, focus loss, backgrounding, finish, restart, and replay transitions explicit enough to avoid multiple active race loops or accumulated real-time jumps.

When the game is paused, in Photo Mode, or when the tab becomes hidden, **freeze active-race simulation, AI, race timers, nitro consumption, environmental progression, and recording consistently** while retaining the instantaneous velocity vectors ($\mathbf{v}, \boldsymbol{\omega}$) of vehicles and camera so Photo Mode can still render authentic velocity-based directional motion blur while time is frozen ($\Delta t = 0$). On resumption, discard stale held inputs where appropriate and restore a usable state without a burst of unintended acceleration, a huge physics step, or several minutes of phantom elapsed race time.

The results screen and replay viewer must not continue an invisible competitive race behind the UI. Replay uses recorded states and time, not a second race simulation accidentally sharing control with the active one. Avoid contradictory copies of the same race result in UI components, save data, and recorder metadata.

## 12. Lap, checkpoint, and position integrity

Track meaningful progress along each designated route, with an ordered checkpoint scheme, forward progression, lap count, and start/finish validation. Technical implementation may use sectors, waypoints, splines, projections, or another representation, but it must handle normal racing and predictable errors.

At minimum, consider these cases: crossing the finish in the wrong direction; oscillating across a line; missing a gate then rejoining later; cutting across a loop; leaving the road; using recovery; two opponents passing close together; and a car reversing into an earlier sector. None may create a free lap, time bonus, impossible classification, or record from an invalid traversal.

Define what counts as legitimate recovery versus a reset that invalidates the current lap or requires rejoining an earlier valid location. Make that choice clear in gameplay. A player should not be punished by an incomprehensible invisible rule, but neither should a recovery button become an optimal shortcut.

Records and result metrics must use validated state. If a run is abandoned, failed, paused, or only partially completed, do not report an invented finished time or award progress that was never reached.

## 13. The garage and four differentiated real-world-informed cars

Supply a working, architecturally detailed and physically lit garage with **at least four original, recognizably different selectable car designs, each informed by the technical proportions, silhouette, and aerodynamic packaging of a distinct real-world automotive archetype while remaining an original, legally clean creative design** (using fictional manufacturer/team names and badges, never 1:1 clones of protected commercial designs). Document in the garage UI and `README.md` the technical archetype and real-world proportional inspiration for each of the four vehicles, and **tailor the aerodynamic and coachwork components to each car's specific architecture** rather than forcing identical prototype fins or wings onto every car:

1. **Car #1 — Le Mans Hypercar / LMDh Endurance Prototype Archetype:** Low-slung prototype proportions, sculpted front and rear pontoon fenders with top wheel-arch vents, deep front splitter, teardrop prototype glass canopy, roof intake snorkel, **central dorsal stabilizing shark fin**, wide dual-element endurance rear wing with endplates, multi-tunnel Venturi rear diffuser, and dual horizontal HDR emissive LED tail-light strips.

2. **Car #2 — Mid-Engine V10/V12 Aerodynamic Exotic Supercar Archetype:** Cab-forward wedge profile, low sculpted nose, side radiator intakes and flying buttresses or slatted glass engine bay cover, **integrated active/sculpted rear aero bridge or low-profile supercar spoiler (no prototype dorsal fin)**, wide rear haunches, and central/high-mounted performance exhausts above an aggressive diffuser.

3. **Car #3 — Widebody Rear/Front-Mid Engine Track GT3 Coupe Archetype:** Distinct production-derived GT fastback coupe cabin and roofline, widebody flared fenders with extractor louvers, front bumper dive planes (canards) and splitter, **high-mounted swan-neck GT3 rear wing (no prototype dorsal fin)**, and endurance-style LED headlight/taillight clusters.

4. **Car #4 — Sculpted Front-Engine V8/V12 Coastal Grand Tourer (GT) Archetype:** Long, muscular sculpted hood with vents/power dome, setback 2-door fastback cabin, sweeping rear fender shoulders, **clean Kammback tail with an integrated ducktail or deployable lip spoiler (no towering track wing or dorsal fin)**, refined grand-touring splitter/diffuser, and quad-exhaust tailpipes.

See Section 45 for mandatory multi-submesh modeling, textured livery, and multi-material shader standards. The selected car must carry through consistently into the race, camera, HUD where relevant, Photo Mode, result, and replay.

Cars must differ materially in physical driving dynamics: mass distribution, drivetrain response, acceleration curve, top speed, speed-dependent aerodynamic downforce and drag, suspension stiffness, cornering grip, weight transfer, braking stability, and drift slip-angle behavior. Tradeoffs must keep **all four** viable and engaging. Avoid offering four recolors or rescaled copies with identical handling, and **never ship a solid-color untextured car**.

A physically lit, detailed car preview in the garage must allow inspecting the vehicle from multiple angles (including front, side, rear, and three-quarter close-ups) and clearly communicate selection, archetype architecture, and truthful comparative physical attributes (power-to-weight, downforce, cornering grip, top speed, drift balance).

## 14. Balanced arcade driving contract and physical depth

Handling must be **approachable on a keyboard or gamepad yet physically deep and expressive**. Steering and acceleration should respond promptly, while velocity, momentum, 4-corner suspension load transfer, road curvature, camber/grade, braking, longitudinal/lateral tire force coupling, aerodynamic downforce, and slip angle matter. Players must feel a clear physical difference between taking a curve at an appropriate speed on the rubbered racing line and attempting the same turn at excessive speed or on wet/off-road surfaces. Recovering from a minor mistake must be possible without hardcore sim-racing hardware.

Maintain internally coherent vehicle dynamics coupling engine torque/RPM/gear ratios, longitudinal traction, braking/reverse, steering geometry, wheel rotation, 4-wheel suspension compression/rebound, chassis pitch/roll, speed-scaled aerodynamic downforce/drag, and yaw dynamics (as detailed in Section 45 and Section 50). Speed and direction must not jump discontinuously without a meaningful event such as collision or deliberate recovery. Avoid purely kinematic 2D movement where a mesh slides along a path ignoring tire traction and surface normals, or a steering system that enables physically nonsensical instant direction reversal at high speed.

Surface and weather effects must alter handling in a controllable, continuous way: dry aggregate asphalt, darker rubbered racing-line groove, wet asphalt, standing puddle zones, painted markings/3D beveled curbs, and off-road shoulders/gravel/sand must produce distinguishable grip, suspension vibration, and braking response for both the player and AI.

The chase camera must frame the road ahead, convey speed and chassis weight transfer, and adapt gently to vehicle motion and elevation changes without clipping through terrain or tunnel walls. Avoid excessive oscillation or a camera that hides approaching bends. Additional live driving camera options are optional; the separate three-camera replay system and free/orbit Photo Mode camera are mandatory.

## 15. Nitro and drift as actual game mechanics

**Nitro** must be activated intentionally, consume a genuinely finite or otherwise bounded resource, and materially increase engine thrust/acceleration with coordinated aerodynamic/exhaust visuals, engine/turbo sound pitch, speed cues, and camera FOV/motion-blur response. A truthful on-screen indicator shows available resource and active state. Recharge and balancing are creative design choices (e.g., recharging via slipstream, clean high-speed driving, or controlled drifting), provided nitro cannot remain permanently free or unlimited during ordinary play.

**Drift** must be a controllable physical-arcade maneuver with intentional entry (via handbrake/drift key or brake-turn weight transfer), a sustained controllable lateral slip-angle and yaw regime governed by coupled longitudinal/lateral tire friction, countersteer stability, and a recoverable exit with real trajectory consequences. It must leave physically connected dual tire marks, smoke (dry), or dense spray (wet) on applicable surfaces, not merely spawn generic particles while ordinary steering continues unchanged.

The two mechanics must feel integrated with racing and the three modes. Interrupting nitro or drift through pause, Photo Mode, collision, recovery, restart, race finish, or a focus change must leave controls and resources consistent. Animation and sound must not remain permanently active after the underlying mechanic ends.

## 16. Collision response, off-track behavior, and recovery

Player and AI cars need contact handling that changes motion or progress sensibly. Vehicle collisions, roadside barriers, guardrails, and major world geometry must not be invisible, visually penetrable by default, or capable of leaving cars permanently trapped during ordinary play. Do not make impacts completely irrelevant simply to protect perfect lap times. Ensure the visual road mesh and physical ground-contact height agree across hills, bridges, curbs, and banking so cars never float above or sink through the road surface.

Collision response must preserve the balanced arcade feel: readable momentum transfer, angular impulse damped for playability, deflection, impact sparks/debris/audio feedback, and localized progressive visual damage, with enough forgiveness to keep the game fun. Use coherent rules for player and AI; opponents must not become invulnerable ghosts whose movement ignores other cars unless a narrowly justified, visibly transparent recovery rejoin window is active.

Provide an off-track or stuck recovery action or automatic recovery path that restores a sensible heading and nearby legitimate location. Recovery must not silently advance checkpoints, award time, teleport directly into an advantageous position, or destroy otherwise valid progress. Both human and AI cars should eventually return to a raceable state after plausible incidents. On recovery, clear residual linear and angular motion and any active nitro/drift state; rejoin with a sensible, non-advantageous restart rather than carrying pre-recovery momentum into a new heading. Avoid spawning a rigid body inside another car or obstacle: choose a clear lateral re-entry location or use a short, clearly indicated non-collision rejoin phase without permitting progress exploits.

### Damage-state relationship

Immediate collision physics and accumulated visual damage are related but different concepts. A single impact may reduce speed and add localized visible damage to the impacted zone (front, rear, left, right); previously recorded damage must not vanish just because the car accelerates again. In this expanded revision, per-component mechanical consequences and a real repair pathway are mandatory under Section 60.3; purely cosmetic damage alone no longer satisfies the complete damage system. Significant localized impacts or mechanical abuse must impair modeled brakes, suspension, transmission and/or aerodynamic components as specified by E03, with corresponding visible/audible feedback and a genuine in-run, player-accessible service/repair workflow. Repair must have causal component-state effects and cannot grant lap/checkpoint progress, erase historical replay damage, or invent a competitive finish. The player must have a viable recovery/service route and never be permanently softlocked by damage.

## 17. Five competent simultaneous AI opponents

The Race mode must run **five concurrently active racing opponents**, each with actual progressing vehicle state, multi-wheel/suspension pose, and correct positions. Give each rival a stable recognizable identity, including a driver name, racing number, and distinguishable multi-color textured racing livery across the four vehicle archetypes, used consistently in the starting grid, live standings, results and replay. The opponents must be visibly governed by the same track boundaries, surface wetness/grip conditions, aerodynamic slipstream, and broad performance envelope as the player.

Implement **deep multi-horizon AI perception and control** (further detailed in Section 46): rivals must evaluate upcoming corner curvature across multiple lookahead distances to compute physical braking points, adjust target speed for wet asphalt and car archetype downforce, perceive nearby cars via spatial/raycast sensors to select inside/outside overtaking or defensive lines, utilize slipstream wakes on straights, yield or brake to avoid pileups, and execute multi-point/reverse or assisted recovery when obstructed or spun.

Opponents must not stack at spawn indefinitely, drive in a rigid single-file train along one spline, freeze when overtaken, become stranded after a small collision, or finish impossible laps through teleports. Difficulty differences (`Easy`, `Normal`, `Hard`) must be perceptible and measurable across valid races without position-based rubber-banding.

## 18. Dynamic weather and changing daylight

Support distinguishable **sunny, cloudy, and rainy** weather and **morning, daytime, sunset, and nighttime** lighting states. Before racing, the player can select the initial weather and time of day. Those choices must meaningfully affect the starting scene, not merely the menu text.

**Both weather and time of day progress during live gameplay** as a coupled world simulation (see Section 47). Transitions must be smooth enough to read as evolving environmental conditions and perceptible within relatively short runs. Do not require every condition to occur in every single race or force an implausibly rapid full day/night cycle. The system can use artistic time compression, controlled transitions or designed environmental patterns, as long as the sky scattering, sun/moon direction, volumetric haze, crepuscular sun rays, cast shadows, **dynamic sky-synchronized environment reflections (IBL)**, scene materials and driving conditions evolve coherently.

As rain begins, grows or recedes, a continuous surface-wetness and puddle-accumulation state (with realistic wetting and post-rain drying hysteresis) must drive road specular reflections, standing puddles, micro-roughness, tire spray, visibility cues, road lighting and **traction/braking behavior** progressively. Surface conditions affect the player and the AI under consistent rules. Driving on a wet section must feel different from an equivalent dry section; a color filter and screen-space streaks alone do not satisfy dynamic weather.

Nighttime driving must remain playable: road edges, reflective road studs/cat's-eyes, upcoming curves, checkpoints, rivals and obstacles are legible using plausible projected vehicle headlights, glowing LED taillights, street/tunnel lighting, and well-designed visual contrast. Weather and daylight updates must not change the race clock, invalidate saved laps, or reset the player's position.

## 19. Progressive visible collision damage

Represent **accumulating, directionally aware visual vehicle damage caused by actual collisions**, with distinguishable degrees of severity and a clear spatial relationship to impacts (front, rear, left, right). Appropriate solutions include localized paint scratches/scuffs across the livery, exposed primer/carbon/metal under the clearcoat, localized panel/bumper/fender denting or displacement, cracked/flickering headlight/taillight optics, wing/splitter misalignment, smoke, sparks or other coherent artistic choices. No exact external mesh-deformation library is mandated, but damage must visibly modify the car's appearance in the impacted zones.

Damage must not appear randomly unrelated to contact, vanish immediately after an impact, or reset between checkpoints of the same race. It must remain visible through the active run and clear on a genuine new run. Cars must remain recoverably playable even after severe damage. **E03 makes mechanical condition mandatory, not optional or cosmetic**: brake, suspension, transmission and aerodynamic impairment must genuinely affect physics under appropriate impacts/abuse, and component repairs must genuinely restore behavior through the in-run service pathway without shortcutting the mandatory race. Damage and collision effects must support the same photoreal artistic style in rain, daylight and night.

A replay must recover the correct accumulated per-zone damage for the replayed instant. Seeking backward to before an impact must remove its subsequent damage; seeking forward after that event must reproduce it. Do not simply retain the final damaged car model for the entirety of the replay.

## 20. Presentation, camera movement, and the sensation of speed

Use a polished third-person chase view for active racing. Frame the drivable route ahead, retain spatial context near tight corners, handle tunnel/obstacle occlusion smoothly, and smooth intentional camera movements without obscuring controls. Scale sense and speed must come from **camera-relative directional motion blur on the asphalt and curbs**, road texture flow, background parallax, car suspension pitch/roll movement, sound pitch/load, controlled field of view, particle feedback, wheel rotation, lighting and other deliberate details rather than constant exaggerated camera shake.

Create coherent vehicle presentation: body posture (pitch dive on braking, squat on acceleration/nitro, lateral roll in corners), wheel steering/spin, contact cues and visual speed feedback must agree with physical motion. Build distinct visual moods for coastal sunlight, golden-hour sunbursts, town driving, tunnel travel and rain/night conditions without losing the game's recognizable palette.

Menus, garage, Photo Mode, both creation tools, loading, HUD, replay, results and all fallback/error screens require the same AAA-inspired visual craft and original design language as gameplay, with the concrete Sections 65–71 UI/UX standards and U01–U16 proofs. Avoid using temporary debug overlays as final interface, unstyled browser defaults everywhere, unreadable tiny text, giant start banners blocking the windshield view, massive bloom that conceals corners, or unrelated effect stacks solely to look complex.

Respect a reduced-motion preference where possible: limit decorative camera travel, disable velocity motion blur when `prefers-reduced-motion` or the in-app Reduced Motion toggle is active, and avoid mandatory flashing effects for critical events.

## 21. Sound, feedback, and UI animation

Provide credible, cohesive local sound feedback for essential racing events through original, procedural/synthesized multi-layer audio or properly licensed bundled resources. Engine note driven by actual RPM, throttle load, and multi-gear transmission state (with distinct acoustic character per car archetype, such as high-revving prototype/V10 whine vs. deep V8/GT3 bark), gear-shift transients, turbo/nitro burst, tire slip squeal on dry asphalt vs. hiss/spray on wet asphalt, rumble-curb vibration buzz when riding the 3D curbs, aerodynamic wind rush, ocean surf, tunnel reverb, impacts, countdown, checkpoints and finish must form a continuous, responsive soundscape (see Section 48). A generic single-frequency oscillator running at constant pitch does not qualify. A musical soundtrack is optional; meaningful physical audio feedback is mandatory. The game must remain usable with sound muted or unavailable.

Respect browser autoplay and user-gesture restrictions. Audio must not generate errors that prevent a race from starting; use a discoverable activation or graceful muted state where necessary. Prevent layered engine sounds, multiple countdown instances, and leaked audio graphs after restarting, leaving a race, entering Photo Mode, opening replay or switching tabs.

HUD and interface animation should clarify state transitions rather than replace working state. Use readable counters, position changes, progress feedback, nitro availability and clear results. Avoid a meter that updates cosmetically while the underlying state remains unchanged.

## 22. True gameplay-state replay: recording requirements

A required replay is a **recording of the actual race in every game mode**, not a canned camera flythrough, an unrelated deterministic demo, prerecorded video, or a collection of snapshots of the UI. Valid finished Time Trials, completed AI races and successful Checkpoint Runs must all yield full replays; a timed-out Checkpoint Run must also yield a replay labeled as a genuine failed run. A player-retired or otherwise abandoned run may produce only an explicitly partial replay. Record sufficient information to reconstruct meaningful race motion and state: the player's vehicle, the five active AI opponents (with their exact identities/cars/liveries), linear and angular velocity vectors (so directional motion blur works accurately in replay and replay-launched Photo Mode), wheel/body/suspension pose, timing and progression, nitro/drift state, collisions, per-zone visual **and E03 mechanical/component condition and repair events**, weather/wetness and daylight evolution, **E01 per-wheel tire thermals/pressure/wear/compound, E02 active aero/wake forces, **immutable value-complete snapshots of E04 tuning and E10 modular vehicle part assemblies (including physical parameters, component geometry/material definitions and versioned asset identifiers), and of E09 edited course geometry/checkpoints/collision when recording an editor playtest**, E05 deformable contact terrain, E06 localized water depth/flooding**, E07/E08 environmental parameters affecting lighting/visibility, and each Signature Feature's visible state or events. Capture keyframes/deterministic inputs/events with sufficient fidelity to reconstruct player and AI physics and historical surfaces without treating GPU presentation buffers as authoritative state.

The implementation is free to use state snapshots, event logs, keyframes, deterministic simulation with correction, or a suitable hybrid. It need not guarantee bit-for-bit identity of transient particle pixels or audio samples. It **must** faithfully show what happened: correct cars, order of events, positions, course progression, visible conditions, Signature Feature effects, and damage at the selected replay instant.

The recording begins and ends at well-defined points in the race lifecycle. It must not capture menu time or Photo Mode pause time as racing time, continue writing indefinitely after finish, or merge two attempts into one replay after restart. A partial recording may be retained only if it is honestly identified and playable as partial; never mislabel an abandoned or corrupt attempt as a completed race.

Do not call a replay complete solely because its file can be parsed. The recorded scene must correspond to a real played run and make sense across all the important events. **Mutable garage, setup and track-editor IDs may be stored as informative labels only, never as the sole source for historical reconstruction.** Replay, export/import and replay-launched Photo Mode must use immutable embedded race-start configuration and course snapshots, with validated size limits, versioned schemas and bundled or safely reconstructible visual resources. Editing, overwriting or deleting a current E04/E09/E10 saved configuration must not retroactively change an old recording.

## 23. Replay viewer: temporal consistency and cinematic control

Provide a distinct replay viewer with **at least three meaningfully different, usable camera perspectives** (including a chase/driver-near action view, an automated cinematic trackside camera system, and a sweeping aerial/helicam or hood perspective), cinematic yet readable framing, play/pause, a visible timeline, seeking forward and backward, and rewinding to an earlier moment. **Photo Mode access from the replay viewer is mandatory, including imported and locally recorded compatible replays. Entry must pause replay playback and exit must restore the exact previous replay timestamp and camera without modifying recorded history.** The viewer must work for a just-completed run and for a valid imported/saved replay.

Seeking may reconstruct state from snapshots or re-evaluate logged events, but the viewer must settle on the **same recorded historical state at a given time regardless of the direction of seeking**. Jumping to a pre-collision moment must restore undamaged presentation; jumping forward to the collision must restore the correct damage. Weather, road wetness, daylight, dynamic IBL reflections, nitro, Signature Feature state, and AI positions must correspond to that replay instant, rather than adopting current live menu settings. Treat discontinuities such as respawn/teleport and rotation-angle wrap-around ($-\pi / +\pi$ or $0^\circ / 360^\circ$) explicitly: interpolation must never fly a car through scenery or turn a small heading change into a full $360^\circ$ spin. Replay HUD values, including elapsed time, lap/checkpoint state and standings, must reflect **the selected historical timestamp**, not the final race state.

Pause, play, scrub, jump to start/end, switch camera, leave and reopen replay without creating duplicate playback loops, changing recorded data, corrupting the active game's state, or causing time to advance while paused. Display useful playback status, duration, and mode/car metadata. Stop at the end rather than drifting into unrecorded motion. Scrubbing, pausing, seeking across collisions, or leaving the viewer must silence/reset transient engine or impact audio and particle emitters as appropriate; historical bursts must not repeatedly accumulate or continue sounding while time is paused.

Replay camera choice may be a viewing preference; it must not modify the recorded physical history. If a short race uses weather changes or AI passes, watching and seeking through those events should remain visually coherent.

## 24. Replay library: five entries, local only

Provide a browseable local library containing **no more than five saved replay entries**. Show meaningful labels or metadata, such as mode, course, car, date, duration and completion status where known; distinguish **locally played** recordings from **imported** ones by stamping every file-ingested entry as **imported**, regardless of origin flags inside the file. An imported file's claimed time, origin or completion status is untrusted metadata, not a verified local race result. Allow opening, deleting, exporting and importing recordings without an account or remote service. **The just-ended run remains available as a transient replay until the player explicitly chooses to save it**; viewing it does not consume a library slot. If leaving that replay or results view would discard the only copy, offer a clear save/export/discard decision or warning. When saved successfully, it can be revisited after a normal refresh when persistent browser storage is available.

A full library must not silently evict the oldest or newest replay. When the player attempts to save or import a sixth, explain the capacity and obtain a user choice to delete or replace an existing entry, or cancel. Do not destroy a previous valid replay until the new recording has been validated and the replacement can be completed safely. Show truthful outcomes for save, delete and import operations.

**Export and import as portable local files are mandatory.** Exported files must contain what is needed for meaningful playback under the intended game version and asset assumptions, rather than only a local storage key or a link to a private browser origin. Export must be user-initiated and work without external upload. Imported compatible recordings must appear in the same replay library or an explicitly temporary viewing flow when persistence is unavailable.

## 25. File format, storage integrity, and graceful degradation

Choose a documented versioned replay representation suitable for local file exchange. Validate incoming schema version, expected structure, numeric bounds, timestamps, sample counts, event types, identifiers, size limits and unreasonable duration/coordinate data before admitting an import. Treat all imported data as untrusted; do not evaluate it as code, load executable payloads, construct arbitrary resource URLs from it, or permit it to alter global settings without an explicit choice. If files are compressed or encoded, apply limits to **both raw input and expanded/parsed content**, including sample/event counts and bounded processing; stop before decompression or parsing can exhaust memory or freeze the tab.

Keep file size, memory cost and playback performance reasonable for short races. The engineering method is open: compression, sparse event records, interpolation and bounded sampling are permitted when they preserve required fidelity. Validate round-trip behavior: an exported replay should be importable into a compatible clean browser session and show the same recorded driving and events.

**No database technology of any kind is permitted for game data**, including IndexedDB, SQLite, cloud databases, database servers, or a disguised database accessed through another API. **Cache Storage and service-worker caches are not permitted for replay or record persistence.** Use small browser-local preference/metadata storage such as `localStorage` when appropriate. For larger replay recordings use ordinary browser file storage such as **OPFS when supported**, or another non-database file-oriented method compatible with these restrictions. OPFS availability, secure-context requirements, persistence policy and quota are browser-dependent; detect capabilities rather than assuming them.

Maintain consistency if multiple tabs or windows of the same origin try to modify replay storage concurrently. Serialize, detect conflicts or refuse one write with a useful message; never exceed the five-entry limit or silently overwrite a newer edit. Recover from interrupted writes and reconcile stale metadata or orphaned recording files on subsequent access without silently deleting valid user recordings. If a valid orphaned recording is found while the library already has five entries, do not silently admit a sixth or discard it: offer an explicit recovery/export, replacement or deletion choice while preserving the capacity invariant. A browser-specific locking mechanism is optional; the observable consistency is mandatory. Render all imported names and labels as bounded plain text, not executable markup; exported replay files must not contain device identifiers, private paths or browser-specific tokens. Imported or replayed recordings may be viewed and re-exported, but **must never create, update or overwrite legitimate personal-best records**, regardless of claimed metadata.

If persistent replay storage is unavailable, denied, or exhausted, **the race must remain playable**. Provide an honest transient replay and/or direct file-export path when possible, explain that the recording is not durably saved, and avoid success labels for failed writes. Browser site-data clearing can erase local records; do not claim otherwise. Validate saved metadata on reload and handle stale entries, incomplete writes or malformed local content without blanking the whole game.

## 26. Menu, garage, settings, Photo Mode access, and race HUD

Create a premium, original, cohesive and complete visual/interaction system rather than a pile of modal dialogs, a website-style dashboard or unstyled default form. Implement and inspect every screen and UI state in Sections 65–71; functional clickability alone is insufficient. Important paths—play, choose mode and one of the four cars, configure conditions, start, pause, enter Photo Mode, restart, finish, replay, manage recordings, and return—should be easy to discover. State changes should be visible immediately and reflected in the actual next race.

The in-race HUD must communicate **speed, RPM/gear or engine load cue, nitro, route/lap progress, and the mode's defining information**: lap times and best valid lap for Time Trial; running position, laps and named competitors for Race; countdown, `Checkpoint X / N` status and arcade score/combos for Checkpoint Run; plus unobtrusive indicators for active Signature Features where applicable. Place information at the periphery with balanced hierarchy so the central roadway and horizon remain unobstructed; implement the distinct, real-state-sourced HUD designs and viewport/rain/night tests required in Section 67. Display countdown, invalid-lap status, collision or recovery feedback, and finish/failure clearly.

Use a shared design language across menus, garage, HUD, Photo Mode, settings, dialogs, results and replay. Build reliable focus, hover/active/disabled, loading, selected and error states. Do not show actionable-looking controls that do nothing. Choose a coherent interface language and use it consistently across core menus, controls, race HUD, results, errors and replay; localization into multiple languages is optional. Settings must cover at minimum **Low/Medium/High graphics quality, internal render resolution/scale, motion-blur intensity/toggle, configurable keyboard bindings, gamepad dead-zone/sensitivity or status, master/effects/music volume and mute, exposure/brightness where supported, captions/event cues, and reduced motion**. Include a practical fullscreen option and frame-rate cap when supported by the target browser; unavailable controls must disclose the limitation rather than pretend to apply. A setting that cannot safely change during a race may apply at the next race if that delay is clearly indicated. Settings should reflect actual runtime state and persisted values; applying quality or bindings must not surprise the player by resetting an unrelated race.

## 27. Keyboard control, rebinding, gamepad integration, and focus ownership

**Keyboard driving is required as the universal primary control path.** At minimum the player can accelerate, brake/reverse, steer left/right, initiate drift, use nitro, trigger active Signature Feature actions where applicable (when a feature uses a direct key trigger), pause, enter Photo Mode (from pause/replay), and request recovery. Provide sensible discoverable defaults. The principal driving actions must be **fully remappable** through the application, with conflict detection, understandable resolution or rejection, reset-to-defaults, and browser-local persistence when available.

Remapping must affect the actual gameplay input handler, not only labels. Store primary bindings by physical key identity (`KeyboardEvent.code`) so non-US layouts such as ABNT2 and AZERTY are usable; display localized key labels where the browser exposes the active layout, with an understandable fallback. Avoid default controls that require four or more simultaneous pressed keys for ordinary driving. Account for keys held before pause, loss of window focus, switching into an editable text field, remapping a key while it is pressed, and simultaneous movement commands. Inputs intended for menu navigation or typing must not accidentally accelerate the hidden vehicle. During active driving, take ownership of gameplay keys: prevent browser scrolling from relevant keys and prevent `Space`/`Enter` or another bound driving key from activating a still-focused HUD button. When transitioning from a menu, move focus to the game surface or otherwise prevent this conflict without breaking intentional menu/typing accessibility. ## 38. Quality bar and acceptance boundaries

A feature is complete only when its **visible interface, player action, game-state effect, visual finish, and result** agree. In particular:

* **Universal material & shader completeness:** A car or world is not photoreal merely because a shader exposes PBR uniforms; **every surface in the rendered world** (cars, tires, glass, carbon, asphalt, curbs, barriers, signs, cliffs, rocks, sand, vegetation, buildings, bridges, tunnels, and ocean) must have multi-channel textured/shader detail (normal, roughness, metalness, AO, albedo breakup) with **zero solid-color substitute materials**, plus dynamic sky-synchronized IBL reflections verified across daylight/sunset/night state changes, directional shadows, volumetric haze, crepuscular sun rays, and camera-relative motion blur verified in real screenshots.

* **Authentic AI competition:** An opponent is not functional merely because an AI-labeled car moves along a spline; all five rivals must participate in valid multi-horizon physical competition with distinct identities and liveries.

* **Physical weather coupling:** Rain is not functional merely because particles and a dark road appear; continuous wetness and puddle hysteresis must alter specular response, spray, driving grip and AI braking.

* **Reversible replay damage:** Damage is not functional merely because an impact creates sparks; the accumulated per-zone vehicle state must be visible on the car body and reversible when scrubbing replay backward.
